The top bar

Play and Stop, undo and redo, Bypass, and Remove the selected chord (a minus, not a bin: Undo puts it back). On the right: the key, the clip's bars, the tempo and a dot that lights while the module plays.

The staff

The progression drawn as notes, the bar numbers below, the playhead running. Every note is a note the module plays: voicings, inversions and rhythms included. Tap a chord to select it and hear it while you hold.

The chord strip

The chords in order, each card as wide as its length, so the strip lines up with the staff. Tap or hold a card to select it and hear it; + adds a copy of the selected chord after it.

The tabs — the six banks

MAIN

The harmony and the clip: scale, key, how many chords, how long the clip is, how the voicings group. Acts on the whole progression.

  • ScaleWhich notes exist. Roots snap to it and it draws the grid's lanes. Live: changing it re-snaps the progression already in the buffer.
  • KeyThe tonic, and the line you read pitch against. Transposes the buffer as you turn it; at the edge of the range it stops rather than deforming.
  • Octave DefaultThe octave the progression sits in. Moves the whole buffer by octaves as you turn it, and sets where a newly filled slot starts.
  • ChordsHow many chords. Growing copies the last one; while the buffer is still empty it instead divides Clip Bars between the slots.
  • New Chord LenThe length a chord is given. It cannot reach a chord you have written — but while the buffer is empty it re-lengths every slot, and the clip follows.
  • Clip BarsHow long the clip is, and the one loop length. It follows the music — both ways — until you turn it yourself; after that it is yours, and only grows if the music would not fit.
  • Voice GroupingHow the chords sit against each other, and where. dynamic leads each voicing from the one before; the registers fold them into two octaves; opens and drops space them.
  • Note LanesWhich grid lines the staff draws: only the notes of the selected scale, or all seven letter names.

START

The libraries to begin from: 142 progressions by genre, 12 common and 13 uncommon, and the voicing colour. Acts on the whole progression.

  • GenreGroups the library into 17 styles, house to cinematic. Turning it jumps to that genre's first progression and applies it — the gesture Family performs on Shape.
  • Progression142 progressions, stored as semitones from the tonic so they land in your Key and Scale. Voiced by style, so Colour leaves them alone. It overwrites.
  • Common ProgThe progressions most songs are built from. Written as plain triads with no style of their own, which is what makes Colour meaningful on them.
  • UncommonChromatic and modal material: borrowed iv, backdoor, Phrygian, chromatic mediants, secondary dominants. Plain triads like Common, so Colour applies.
  • ColourHow the chord is voiced, not which chord it is: close, open, the drops, shell, rootless, quartal, spread, cluster. The axis Shape cannot express.

CHORD

The selected chord: its degree or root, its octave, its shape, inversion, length, offset and velocity. Acts on the selected chord.

  • DegreeSets the chord's root by scale degree, counted through the scale's own notes — a pentatonic has five, a blues six. Bound to the pad row, where a missing degree is dark.
  • RootThe chord's root note; Family and Shape build upward from it. A root you name here is exact: the Scale moves only roots chosen by degree.
  • Chord OctWhich octave that root sits in. Moves the whole chord with its shape and inversion intact, leaving the voicing alone.
  • Shape FamilyNot a value of its own: it reads the chord's family and writes Chord Shape. Leaving a family and coming back lands on that family's first chord.
  • Chord ShapeThe chord itself, from intervals through 13ths — the one thing stored per chord and written into the clip. Includes rest.
  • InversionOne ladder through zero: up rotates the lowest voice an octave up, down rotates the highest an octave down. Each detent moves exactly one voice.
  • LengthHow long this chord lasts, in eighths of a bar — an absolute duration, so 8 is one bar whatever New Chord Len says. Chords run back to back.
  • OffsetDelays the chord inside its own slot so it can sit off the beat. It never pushes the next chord along.
  • Chord VelOffsets this chord's velocity against the global Velocity. Visible in the grid: a quieter chord draws lighter.

VOICE

The selected chord's voicing and feel: colour, strum, gate, mute, rhythm, transpose. Acts on the selected chord.

  • ColourHow the chord is voiced, not which chord it is: close, open, the drops, shell, rootless, quartal, spread, cluster. The axis Shape cannot express.
  • StrumSpreads this chord's notes across time, lowest first — a strum rather than every note landing together.
  • Chord GateOffsets how much of this chord's length actually sounds, against the global Gate. Shorter reads as a stab.
  • MuteSilences this chord but keeps it in the progression. Drawn as an outline, so you can still see what it would play.
  • Rhythm35 figures — the grid, syncopation, clave, jazz comping, rock, house stabs, cinematic pulses. Anchored to the BAR and repeating across the chord; hold strikes once and is held.
  • TransposeTransposes this chord in semitones after its shape is built, so the shape survives and only the pitch moves.

FEEL

The whole progression's feel: velocity, gate, swing and the humanising, with its dice. Acts on the whole progression.

  • VelocityBase velocity that every chord is measured from. The per-chord Chord Vel is an offset against it.
  • GateBase proportion of a chord's length that actually sounds. The per-chord Chord Gate offsets this one.
  • SwingLeans the off-beat at the finest level the figure uses: odd 16ths under a 16th figure, the ands under an eighth one. Anchored to the bar.
  • Human VelSpreads velocity per note, as a share of the base. Seeded, so the same feel repeats until you randomize it.
  • Randomize VelRe-rolls which deviations the notes get. Same depth, a new feel — and Stamp writes the take you heard.
  • Human TimeSpreads onsets per note and per hit. The spread is shifted so the earliest note still lands on the beat, never clamped.
  • Randomize TimeRe-rolls the timing spread and leaves velocity where it is, so the two can be dialled in separately.

CLIP

The patterns, and what you do to the clip while it plays: transpose, octave, read, stamp, run, and the length gestures. Acts on the whole progression.

  • PatternsSixteen pattern slots, and you are always in one. Tap a slot to go there (an empty one is where the next pattern is written); hold one to copy what you play into it; DELETE, then a tap, empties one. Gold is the slot you are in, blue holds music.
  • Clip TransposeMoves everything that sounds by semitones, Stamp included. Key, Scale and the degree pads stay as written; the staff names the chords that sound.
  • Clip OctaveMoves everything that sounds by whole octaves, for performing. What you wrote is untouched, so turning back to 0 is exactly where you were.
  • Read ClipReads the clip playing on the track Read Track names, which follows the track you select on Move, and names its chords so you can edit what you recorded.
  • Read ModeWhether reading replaces the progression or appends to it, so one phrase can be built from several clips.
  • Arm 1 lapArms one lap for Move to record: press Record on Move (with Stacks still on screen, or after Back), and it plays the progression once on Move's downbeat.
  • ClearEmpties the buffer to a single rest, in your Key and Octave Default — not to a demo progression.
  • RunWhether Stacks plays along with Move's transport. Switched off after a stamp so the clip is not doubled.
  • DuplicateRepeats the whole progression at the same tempo, growing the clip to hold it. Refused if it would not fit.
  • HalftimeHalftime: every chord held twice as long, so the phrase plays at half speed over twice the bars. Bound to hold Copy; a tap duplicates instead.
  • Double-timeDouble-time: every chord half as long. The exact inverse of Half Length, so the pair round-trips; refused at the floor rather than flattening the relative lengths.
  • UndoSteps back through the buffer. Every write is captured, so an ordinary shape edit undoes like a Duplicate does.
